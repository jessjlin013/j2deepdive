Landscape: Inner Logic and its competitors

Inner Logic is already backed by J2, so these companies are context, not picks. They aren't scored or ranked above.

Where Inner Logic leads

  • Built for physical AI, not only device testing. adsilico and Virtonomy pitch virtual patients for checking how a device performs. Inner Logic also trains and validates autonomous surgical systems, and its team has moved orthopedic trauma models trained only in simulation onto real hardware.
  • A clinical-plus-robotics team. A neurosurgeon CEO with Johns Hopkins computer vision and surgical robotics researchers, including the robotics lead whose robot performed the first autonomous laparoscopic surgery in a live animal.
  • U.S.-based. Both direct competitors are European. Being close to the FDA and to U.S. defense medicine buyers matters for J2's dual-use thesis.

Where it is exposed

  • Less capital than its closest rival. adsilico has already raised a Series A; Inner Logic is at seed.
  • An open, government-funded testbed. If Kitware's ARPA-H simulator becomes the free standard for endovascular autonomy, Inner Logic has to win on data quality and regulatory-grade evidence, not on access to simulation.
  • Unproven regulatory value. No public device submission has used Inner Logic's evidence yet. The same question applies to every company here, so the first to get simulation evidence accepted by the FDA gains a lasting edge.

How the scores work

Team: clinical and technical depth. Moat: proprietary data, hardware or validated models. Dual-use pull: a named DoD or federal program, contract or clear field-care use. Commercial: who pays outside government. Regulatory: progress toward clearance (5 = cleared, Breakthrough, or not a regulated device). Stage fit: whether J2 could plausibly lead or join the next round (5 = seed or earlier). Each is a 1–5 judgment from public sources only, to adjust after founder and expert conversations. Flagged entries (⚑) are outside the U.S. Landscape entries are competitors to Inner Logic or later-stage comparisons, shown for context and left out of the ranking.
